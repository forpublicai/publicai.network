# Should We Apply? Executive Summary

**Yes, we should apply for Track 1 (Proof of Concept).** The K-12 AI Infrastructure Program RFP aligns strongly with the Public AI Libraries Project's mission to provide accessible, community-based AI infrastructure. A Track 1 application ($100K-$150K for 9 months) would allow us to extend our library pilot program into K-12 schools through a "Library-School AI Bridges" proof-of-concept that creates public goods (datasets, benchmarks, and tools) demonstrating how library AI workstations can support formative assessment for students with limited home technology access. We qualify as a U.S.-based project with established partnerships (Library of Congress, Metagov, Public Knowledge, state libraries) and operational capacity through our Fall 2025 library pilot program. The key deadline is **March 8, 2026, Midnight PST** (32 days away), with a recommended June 1, 2026 start date. The RFP anticipates 4-8 awards ranging from $50K-$250K for 6-12 month projects focused on creating openly-licensed public goods for K-12 formative assessment.

**To maximize competitiveness, we must immediately (1) partner with an education research institution with formative assessment expertise to fill our learning sciences gap, (2) secure commitment from 2-3 pilot library sites willing to collect multimodal student interaction data (voice, drawings, text) with IRB approval, and (3) obtain school district MOUs for library-school partnerships.** The RFP emphasizes "targeted universalism" - serving all students while centering those furthest from opportunity - which perfectly matches our focus on students who rely on libraries for technology access. Our proposal should frame library-based formative assessment data as a novel public good that enables educational app developers and researchers to understand and address equity gaps in AI-enhanced education. Key risks include the tight timeline, need for student data IRB approval, and lack of in-house formative assessment expertise, but these are manageable with the right education research partner and our existing library infrastructure. This represents a natural evolution of our work with strong potential for scaling through 16,000+ U.S. public libraries if the proof-of-concept succeeds, positioning us for larger Track 2 funding in future RFPs.
