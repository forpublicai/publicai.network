# K-12 AI Infrastructure RFP Analysis for Public AI Libraries Project

## Executive Summary

**Should we apply?** Yes, with strategic focus. The Public AI Libraries Project has a compelling opportunity to extend its library-based public AI infrastructure model into K-12 schools through a Track 1 proof-of-concept application.

**Do we qualify?** Yes. As a U.S.-based project with established partnerships (Library of Congress, Metagov, Public Knowledge, state libraries), the project meets eligibility requirements. The existing library pilot program provides relevant assets and expertise to demonstrate capacity for K-12 expansion.

**Key deadline:** March 8, 2026, Midnight PST (32 days from today)

**Recommended funding range:** $100,000-$150,000 for 9-month proof of concept

---

## Detailed Analysis

### 1. Strategic Fit & Alignment

**Strong alignments:**
- **Public goods mission**: Both programs prioritize open, accessible infrastructure serving underserved populations
- **Infrastructure approach**: Libraries as trusted community hubs align with RFP's emphasis on "publicly accessible" and "publicly accountable" AI
- **Multimodal emphasis**: RFP seeks multimodal formative assessment (speaking, drawing, writing) - library workstations could pilot these modalities in school-library partnerships
- **Targeted Universalism**: RFP explicitly seeks solutions for "U.S. students furthest from opportunity" - matches library mission of universal access
- **Existing partnerships**: State library systems (Utah, New Jersey) often have K-12 connections through school library networks

**Areas requiring development:**
- **Formative assessment focus**: RFP specifically targets K-12 formative assessment - libraries would need to articulate clear educational assessment use cases
- **Technical deliverables**: Must produce datasets, benchmarks, or models as public goods - not just deployment infrastructure
- **Learning sciences grounding**: Need to demonstrate education research expertise or partnerships

### 2. Qualification Assessment

**Eligibility: ✓ QUALIFIED**
- U.S.-based project with established organizational structure
- Partnerships include eligible lead organizations (e.g., Metagov is 501(c)(3))
- Can apply as collaborative partnership

**Capacity: STRONG but needs augmentation**

*Existing strengths:*
- Operational infrastructure through library pilot program
- Established privacy and community trust frameworks
- Experience deploying AI workstations in public settings
- Access to diverse communities across pilot sites
- Partnership with major institutions (Library of Congress)

*Gaps to address:*
- Limited K-12 formative assessment expertise (need education research partner)
- No existing K-12 student interaction datasets
- Need stronger learning sciences representation on team
- Unclear AI model development capacity for educational contexts

**Assets: MODERATE**
- Library pilot program begins Fall 2025 (before proposed June 2026 start)
- 6-8 library sites could collect pilot data
- Existing MOUs, privacy policies, and data governance frameworks
- Open source model experience through workstation deployments

### 3. Recommended Approach: Track 1 Proof of Concept

**Track 1 is the right fit because:**
1. Library-to-school extension is innovative but unproven for formative assessment
2. 6-12 month timeline aligns with library pilot program timeline
3. Lower funding ($50K-$250K) appropriate for proof-of-concept
4. Can demonstrate feasibility for larger Track 2 application in future RFPs
5. Focus on "tackling key challenges" rather than comprehensive solution

**Proposed concept: "Library-School AI Bridges for Equitable Formative Assessment"**

Create proof-of-concept public goods demonstrating how public library AI infrastructure can support K-12 formative assessment for students with limited home technology access.

**Deliverables (Track 1 appropriate):**
1. **Dataset**: Multimodal student interaction data from library-based learning sessions (anonymized, with IRB approval)
   - Focus: Students using library AI workstations for homework help, reading comprehension, or skill practice
   - Multimodal elements: voice interactions, drawings/diagrams, written responses
   - Privacy-preserving collection methods that could scale

2. **Benchmark prototype**: Initial evaluation metrics for library-mediated AI formative assessment
   - Component: Equity of access and quality across demographic groups
   - Component: Educator utility of insights from library-based learning

3. **Tools/Process documentation**: Replicable methods for deploying formative assessment AI in community spaces
   - Privacy frameworks for student data in public settings
   - Partnership models between schools and libraries
   - Community trust-building processes

**Why this works:**
- Leverages existing library pilot (no new infrastructure needed)
- Addresses RFP priorities: multimodal, targeted universalism, educator support
- Creates genuinely novel public goods (library-based student data is understudied)
- Proof of concept could scale through 16,000+ U.S. public libraries
- Demonstrates "reaching students furthest from opportunity" (those relying on library tech)

### 5. Strategic Advice for Application

#### A. Build the right team immediately
**Critical addition: Education research partner**
- Seek university education department with formative assessment expertise
- Look for researchers studying learning sciences + educational technology
- Ideal: Someone already studying library-based learning or equity in education
- Suggested institutions: Learning sciences programs at Northwestern, Indiana University, UC Berkeley School of Education, Vanderbilt Peabody College

**Additional expertise needed:**
- Learning scientist specializing in formative assessment
- Education data scientist with K-12 experience
- IRB expert for student data collection
- School librarian or K-12 educator as co-investigator

#### B. Frame around targeted universalism
**RFP explicitly requires "Targeted Universalism" approach:**
- Universal goal: All students need quality formative assessment
- Targeted: Students without home tech rely on libraries for educational AI access
- Emphasize: Rural students, low-income students, students in under-resourced schools
- Leverage existing library data on community demographics served

**Narrative should highlight:**
- Libraries already serve as "homework help centers" in many communities
- School librarians often bridge public library and school systems
- After-school and summer learning in libraries reaches opportunity gaps
- Public library usage data shows who lacks home internet/AI access

#### C. Design data collection thoughtfully
**Privacy is paramount:**
- IRB approval required (factor into budget and timeline)
- Student data is highly sensitive - demonstrate robust data governance
- Anonymization and de-identification methods must be rigorous
- Consider synthetic data generation approaches to protect privacy while enabling public goods

**Multimodal data strategy:**
- Voice: Students explaining their reasoning (RFP emphasizes spoken communication)
- Visual: Drawings of math problems, concept maps, diagram annotations
- Text: Written responses and chat interactions with AI
- Interaction: Keystroke and clickstream patterns showing learning process

**Partnership approach:**
- Get school district MOUs early (include in application as commitment letters)
- Align with schools that already partner with libraries
- Focus on 2-3 pilot library sites with strong school connections
- Ensure educator involvement in design and evaluation

#### D. Emphasize scalability and replicability
**Track 1 should prove concept can become Track 2 or larger:**
- Document processes so other libraries can replicate
- Create toolkit for library-school AI partnerships
- Show how initial proof of concept could scale through library networks
- Position for follow-on funding in future RFPs

**Dissemination strategy:**
- Release through program repository (required)
- Also release through library networks (ALA, state library associations)
- Present at both education and library conferences
- Create accessible documentation for non-technical library staff

#### E. Budget strategically
**Recommended: $100,000-$150,000**

Sample budget allocation:
- **Staff time (40%)**: Project director (20% FTE), education researcher (25% FTE), data scientist (15% FTE), library coordinators
- **Consulting (15%)**: Learning scientist advisor, privacy/IRB legal support, school librarian consultants
- **Equipment/Compute (10%)**: Data storage, annotation tools, privacy-compliant analytics
- **Subawards (20%)**: To education research partner institution
- **Travel (5%)**: Site visits to pilot libraries, presentation of findings
- **IRB and legal (5%)**: Privacy review, data sharing agreements, IRB fees
- **Overhead (5%)**: Well below 15% maximum to demonstrate cost-efficiency

**Do NOT request maximum unless justified** - RFP states cost-efficiency is a review criterion.

#### F. Address evaluation criteria explicitly

**Criterion 1: Significance (most important)**
- How will your public good enable breakthrough improvements in formative assessment?
- Who will use it? (Answer: Educational app developers, model trainers, equity researchers)
- Why will it be adopted? (Answer: Unique library-based data fills gap in understanding equitable AI access)
- How does it serve "students furthest from opportunity"? (Answer: Centers students without home tech access)

**Criterion 2: Assets and Capabilities**
- Describe library pilot program as existing asset
- Detail partnerships with state libraries and Library of Congress
- Acknowledge gaps and explain how education research partner fills them
- Provide existing privacy policies and MOUs as supplemental materials

**Criterion 3: Project Workplan**
- 9-month timeline with clear milestones
- Months 1-2: IRB approval, school partnerships, instrumentation setup
- Months 3-6: Data collection at pilot sites
- Months 7-8: Analysis, annotation, dataset preparation
- Month 9: Documentation, release preparation, final deliverables
- Show rapid start capability (library sites already operational)

**Criterion 4: Release and Dissemination Plan**
- CC-BY-4.0 license for dataset
- Apache 2.0 for any code/tools
- FAIR principles compliance (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable)
- Describe limitations (e.g., K-12 instructional use only, not consumer products)
- Quality assurance process with program team

### 6. Risks and Mitigation

**Risk: Timeline pressure (32 days to write proposal)**
- *Mitigation*: Start immediately, use existing library pilot materials, engage education partner ASAP

**Risk: Lack of formative assessment expertise**
- *Mitigation*: Bring on credible education research partner as co-PI

**Risk: IRB approval timeline may delay data collection**
- *Mitigation*: Budget for expedited IRB review, prepare protocols during application phase, start IRB immediately upon award

**Risk: School district partnerships take time**
- *Mitigation*: Focus on districts that already partner with pilot libraries, get commitment letters now

**Risk: Student data privacy concerns**
- *Mitigation*: Emphasize existing library privacy expertise, engage legal experts, propose conservative data practices

**Risk: Dataset may be too small for Track 1**
- *Mitigation*: Frame as proof-of-concept for methods, not comprehensive dataset; focus on multimodal novelty

### 7. Success Indicators

**Strong application would demonstrate:**
- Clear bridge from library infrastructure to K-12 formative assessment
- Partnership between library expertise and education research expertise
- Unique contribution to public goods (library-based student data is understudied)
- Feasible workplan with operational pilot sites ready to go
- Robust privacy and ethical data practices
- Scalability potential through library networks
- Genuine service to underserved students

**Signs this application will be competitive:**
- Addresses multimodal emphasis (not just text-based AI)
- Centers students without home technology access
- Proposes novel public goods that others would actually use
- Shows operational readiness (sites ready, partnerships in place)
- Team combines library, education, and technical expertise
- Budget is reasonable and cost-efficient

## Conclusion

**Recommendation: PROCEED WITH APPLICATION**

The Public AI Libraries Project should pursue a Track 1 proof-of-concept application focused on extending library AI infrastructure to support K-12 formative assessment for students with limited home technology access. This represents a natural evolution of the library pilot program with strong alignment to RFP priorities.

**Critical next steps (this week):**
1. Identify and contact education research partner with formative assessment expertise
2. Engage 2-3 pilot library sites to gauge interest in K-12 data collection
3. Contact school districts served by pilot libraries about partnership
4. Assign proposal writing lead and assemble core writing team
5. Review IRB requirements and prepare for student data protocols

**Why this is worth pursuing:**
- Genuinely extends Public AI Libraries mission into education sector
- Creates novel public goods (library-based formative assessment data)
- Could unlock library networks as K-12 AI equity solution at scale
- Positions project for larger funding in subsequent RFPs
- Aligns with multiple funders' interests (education, libraries, AI equity)
- Relatively modest lift given existing pilot program infrastructure

The tight timeline is challenging but manageable given existing assets. The key is moving immediately to secure the education research partnership and confirm pilot site participation.
